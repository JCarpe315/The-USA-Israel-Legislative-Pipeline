### 1.1 AIPAC — The Federal Pipeline

The American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC) is a 501(c)(4) organization that explicitly states it "lobbies Congress to pass annual U.S. security assistance to Israel, support lifesaving missile defense cooperation, and fund joint programs." It claims 6.5 million members and operates the largest pro-Israel PAC in the United States.

AIPAC does not merely advocate for bills. It **drafts** them.

**Case 1: Israel Anti-Boycott Act (2017)**  
H.R. 1697 (Rep. Peter Roskam, R-IL) and S. 720 (Sen. Ben Cardin, D-MD) were introduced on March 23, 2017. The Jewish Telegraphic Agency reported explicitly that the bill "was drafted with the assistance of the American Israel Public Affairs Committee." AIPAC identified passage of this bill as one of its top lobbying priorities for 2017.

**Case 2: Combating BDS Act (2019)**  
S. 1, introduced by Sen. Marco Rubio (R-FL) on the **first day** of the 116th Congress (January 3, 2019), was AIPAC's "top legislative priority." AIPAC circulated a memo to all members of Congress defending the bill. The bill was part of a four-bill Middle East package co-sponsored by Sens. James Risch (R-ID), Cory Gardner (R-CO), and Mitch McConnell (R-KY).

**Case 3: Stand with Israel Act (2026)**  
H.R. 7018 (Rep. Mike Lawler, R-NY) and S. 1521 (Sen. James Risch, R-ID) are endorsed by AIPAC, JINSA, FDD Action, the Republican Jewish Coalition, the Combat Antisemitism Movement, ZOA, Christians United for Israel (CUFI), Heritage Action, and EMET. The bill is explicitly modeled after the existing prohibition on funding UN entities that elevate Palestinian Authority status. S. 1521 has **27 Republican co-sponsors** including John Barrasso, Marsha Blackburn, John Boozman, Katie Britt, Ted Budd, Shelley Moore Capito, Susan Collins, John Cornyn, Tom Cotton, Mike Crapo, Joni Ernst, Deb Fischer, Lindsey Graham, Bill Hagerty, Josh Hawley, John Hoeven, James Justice, James Lankford, Mike Lee, Cynthia Lummis, David McCormick, Ashley Moody, Pete Ricketts, Mike Rounds, Rick Scott, and Thom Tillis.

**Case 4: United States–Israel Defense Partnership Act (2025)**  
H.R. 1229 and S. 554 are promoted on AIPAC's bill summaries page. The House version is authored by Reps. Joe Wilson (R-SC) and Donald Norcross (D-NJ); the Senate version by Sens. Dan Sullivan (R-AK), Gary Peters (D-MI), Richard Blumenthal (D-CT), Pete Ricketts (R-NE), and Jacky Rosen (D-NV). The bill establishes a U.S.-Israel Counter-Unmanned Systems Program authorized at $150 million annually through FY2030, extends the "Future of Warfare" emerging technologies program at $50 million annually through FY2030, extends anti-tunnel and counter-UAS cooperation through FY2028 with $20 million increases each, extends the War Reserve Stockpile Authority-Israel through January 1, 2029, directs the Pentagon to establish a Defense Innovation Unit office in Israel, and directs engagement on adding Israel to the National Technology and Industrial Base (NTIB).

### 2.5 Congressional Travel — The Indirect Pipeline

### 3.6 Defense Contractor Profits — The Industrial Base Pipeline

The money does not stop at campaigns. It flows to defense contractors who manufacture the weapons sold to Israel.

**Total U.S. Military Aid to Israel (1959-2024):** According to the Watson Institute for International & Public Affairs at Brown University, total U.S. military aid to Israel accounted for **$251.2 billion** when adjusted for inflation to 2024 dollars.

**Pentagon Contracts (2020-2024):** Private firms received **$2.4 trillion** in contracts from the Pentagon, of which **$771 billion** went to just five firms:
- **Lockheed Martin:** ~40% of contract value (high-priced jets)
- **RTX (formerly Raytheon):** ~19% of contract value
- **Boeing:** ~15% of contract value
- **General Dynamics:** Significant share
- **Northrop Grumman:** Significant share

**Israel-Specific Contracts:**
- **Iron Dome:** Co-production between Raytheon and Rafael (Israeli firm). U.S. has invested over $2 billion in Iron Dome since 2011.
- **David's Sling:** Joint U.S.-Israel program. Raytheon and Rafael partnership.
- **Arrow-3:** Joint Boeing-Israel Aerospace Industries program.
- **F-35 Joint Strike Fighter:** Lockheed Martin. Israel has purchased 50+ F-35s at ~$100M each.
- **KC-46 Aerial Refueling:** Boeing. Israel requested expedited delivery.
- **GBU-43/B MOAB (Massive Ordnance Air Blast):** Boeing. Amendment 52 to H.R. 8034 sought to ensure Israel could obtain this weapon.
- **2,000-pound bombs, JDAMs, tank rounds:** General Dynamics, Raytheon, Northrop Grumman.

**The Co-Production Requirement:** Since 2009, Israel has received $3.9 billion annually in missile defense funding, of which 25% could be spent on Israeli-origin systems. This is set to decrease to zero by 2028 — meaning all future aid must be spent on U.S.-origin systems. This creates a **captured market** for U.S. defense contractors. The FY2026 NDAA and H.R. 4016 explicitly authorize co-production in the U.S. and Israel, ensuring U.S. firms retain control of technology while Israeli firms get manufacturing contracts.

**The FUTURES Act (Section 224/219/1217):** This provision establishes an "executive agent" to synchronize U.S.-Israel defense technology cooperation across 10 domains. The New Republic noted: **"No other country has this privilege with the U.S. military."** This is integration typically reserved for Five Eyes intelligence partners, but without any treaty. It ensures a permanent, institutionalized revenue stream for defense contractors on both sides.

## PART VI: FLOOR MECHANICS

### 6.1 Method 1: Suspension of the Rules
- Used for H.Res. 771 (412–10) and many pro-Israel resolutions.
- Requires 2/3 majority but limits debate to 40 minutes and bars amendments.
- **H.R. 1422 (Enhanced Iran Sanctions Act)** passed by **voice vote under suspension** on March 14, 2026, with 277 cosponsors. This is a procedural lane typically reserved for measures with overwhelming cross-party support. The bill had 162 Republican and 115 Democratic cosponsors, spanning from Lauren Boebert to Steny Hoyer.

### 6.2 Method 2: NDAA Riders
- Israel provisions attached to the National Defense Authorization Act, which has passed for 64 consecutive years.
- Members cannot vote against Israel aid without voting against troop pay.
- The FUTURES Act was inserted as Section 224 of the House NDAA FY2027.
- H.R. 340 (Hamas Financing) passed the House as an NDAA rider but was stripped by the Senate.

### 6.3 Method 3: Omnibus / Supplemental Appropriations
- The $26 billion Israel Security Supplemental was folded into H.R. 815, a national security supplemental that also included Ukraine and Taiwan aid.
- Forces an up-or-down vote on the entire package — no amendments, no line-item vetoes.
- The House held separate votes on Israel (366–58), Ukraine (311–112), and Indo-Pacific (385–34) on April 20, 2024, then recombined them into a single bill for the Senate.
- H.R. 1968 (P.L. 119-4) extended loan guarantees to Israel through 2030 as part of a full-year continuing appropriations act.

### 6.4 Method 4: Discharge Petitions (Rare, Used Against)
- S.J.Res. 32–34, 138, 41, 70 (disapproving arms sales to Israel) were brought to the floor via discharge petitions.
- All failed. S.J.Res. 32 (bulldozers): 40-59. S.J.Res. 138 (bombs): 36-63. S.J.Res. 34: 24-73. S.J.Res. 41: 27-70.
- Notable Democratic "No" votes on S.J.Res. 32: Richard Blumenthal, Chris Coons, Catherine Cortez Masto, John Fetterman, Kirsten Gillibrand, Jacky Rosen, Chuck Schumer.
- Notable Democratic "No" votes on S.J.Res. 138: Added Gary Peters, Jack Reed, Mark Warner, Sheldon Whitehouse.
